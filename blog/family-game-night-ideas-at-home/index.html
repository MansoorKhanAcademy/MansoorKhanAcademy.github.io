10 Family Game Night Ideas at Home (No Screens Needed)

By Mansoor Khan, Mansoor Khan Academy · 2026-11-19

A good family game night does not need a console or expensive board games. Paper, a printer and a few minutes of preparation are enough. Here are ten ideas that work for mixed ages, from young children to grandparents.

1. Guess Who with your own family

This classic guessing game becomes much more fun when the characters are people you know. Type the names of relatives, friends or classmates onto a personalized Guess Who board, print one board for each player and take turns asking yes or no questions. Fold down a tile each time you rule someone out.

2. Who am I? clue cards

Each card has three clues and an answer. One player reads the clues one at a time and the others guess. Fewer clues used means more points. Who am I clue cards let you write clues about people in your own family, which makes everyone laugh.

3. Twenty questions with yes or no cards

Put a set of yes or no question cards in a bowl. A player draws a card and uses it to narrow down the secret person or object. The cards also help shy players, because the question is already written for them.

4. Baby photo quiz

Ask every adult for a childhood photo. Print each photo on a card with a funny hint and let everyone write a guess. It works well at Eid gatherings, weddings and birthday parties.

5. Team tournament with a score sheet

Split into teams and play several rounds of the games above. A score sheet with a total column makes it easy to find the winner without arguments.

6. Draw and guess

One player draws a word on paper while the others guess. Use words from a theme, such as foods, animals or places in your city.

7. Memory tray

Place ten small objects on a tray, show them for 30 seconds, cover the tray, and ask everyone to write down as many as they remember.

8. Treasure hunt with paper clues

Hide small prizes around the house and write rhyming clues on cards. Older children can write clues for the younger ones.

9. Family quiz

Write ten questions about your family history, for example "Where was Grandfather born?" Winners get a small prize, and the children learn real stories.

10. Charades with themes

Use themes such as animals, jobs or movies. Keep the rules simple and let the youngest child choose the first word.

Quick tips

  • Keep each game short, about 10 to 15 minutes, and switch before anyone gets bored.
  • Decide the prize before you start. Even a small treat adds excitement.
  • Let children help prepare the cards. They enjoy the game more when they made part of it.
  • Print on plain paper first. You can reprint on thicker paper if you want cards that last.

Frequently asked questions

How many people can play Guess Who?

The classic version is for two players. With printed boards you can run a tournament where each pair plays and the winners meet in the next round.

Can I print the games at home?

Yes. Download the PNG or PDF and print on A4 or US Letter paper. Cardstock gives a firmer card, but ordinary paper works.

What age is suitable?

Children from about five can play with help. Older children and adults enjoy the clue and question games, especially when the names come from their own family.
