How to Make a Monthly Budget That Works (With Free Preview Printables)

By Mansoor Khan, Mansoor Khan Academy · 2026-11-19

A budget is simply a plan for your money before you spend it. Many people give up on budgets because they make them too complicated. This method uses one page for the plan, one page for tracking and one page for bills, and it takes about 30 minutes to set up.

Step 1: Write down your monthly income

List every source of income for the month: salary, business income, rent and any regular help. Use the amount you are sure to receive, not the amount you hope for. The monthly budget planner has a section for this at the top.

Step 2: List your fixed expenses

Fixed expenses are the same every month: rent, school fees, internet, loan instalments. Write the amount and the due date next to each. A bill payment tracker helps you tick off each bill as you pay it, so none is forgotten.

Step 3: Estimate your variable expenses

Groceries, fuel, mobile credit and transport change every month. Look at last month's spending to estimate. If you do not know, track your spending for one month first with an expense tracker.

Step 4: Decide how much to save first

Do not wait to see what is left at the end of the month. Choose a small amount to put aside as soon as income arrives, even if it is only a few hundred rupees. If you want a game-like approach, try the 52-week savings challenge, where you save a little more each week.

Step 5: Plan to pay down debts

If you have debts, list them with the balance, the minimum payment and the due date. Many people pay the smallest debt first for motivation, and others pay the one with the highest interest first. A debt payoff tracker lets you see the balance go down.

Step 6: Review each week

Spend five minutes every weekend. Check what you spent, move money between categories if needed and note any surprise costs. A budget works because you adjust it, not because it is perfect the first time.

Quick tips

  • Start simple. Three categories are enough in the first month.
  • Keep the printed page where you will see it, such as on the fridge.
  • Include a small amount for surprises so one unexpected cost does not ruin the plan.
  • If you share finances with a partner, review the budget together once a month.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I save each month?

A common starting point is 10 percent of income, but any regular amount is better than none. Choose what you can keep up for several months.

Do I need a spreadsheet?

No. A printed planner works well and many people find it easier to stay consistent with paper.

Is this financial advice?

No. These printables only help you organise your own information. For large decisions such as investments or loans, speak to a qualified financial adviser.
