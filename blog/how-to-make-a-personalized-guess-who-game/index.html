How to Make a Personalized Guess Who Game at Home

By Mansoor Khan, Mansoor Khan Academy · 2026-11-19

A personalised Guess Who game is a great gift and a fun party activity. The idea is simple: each player has a board of characters, secretly chooses one, and asks yes or no questions to find out who the other player picked. Here is how to make your own version with names that mean something to your group.

Step 1: Choose your characters

Decide who will be on the board. Good choices are family members for a family night, classmates for a school activity, or colleagues for a team event. You need 24 names for a full board.

Step 2: Open the editor and type the names

Open the Guess Who board in the studio, then type one name per line in the names box. The board updates as you type, so you can see the result at once. You can change colours and the font until you like the look.

Step 3: Make the secret person cards

Each player needs a hidden card to show who they chose. The character cards page has eight cards per page, so print it, cut the cards out and place them face down in a pile.

Step 4: Prepare the question list

The best questions have a clear yes or no answer, for example "Does your person wear glasses?" or "Is your person older than 40?". Print a set of question cards so younger players have help. There are themes for family, kids, classroom, office and Eid.

Step 5: Print and cut

Print each board on A4 paper. Use plain paper for the first try. If you want to reuse the board, print on thicker paper or put it inside a plastic sleeve and use small stickers or coins to cover ruled out tiles.

Step 6: Play

Each player takes one board and one secret card. Take turns asking one question. After every answer, cover the tiles that no longer fit. The first player to name the other player's person correctly wins. Use a score sheet if you play several rounds.

Quick tips

  • Use first names only if you share the boards publicly.
  • For small children, use 12 names and cover the extra tiles.
  • If you add photos, ask permission first from the people in them.
  • A clear, simple question works better than a clever one.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to draw the characters?

No. The template draws friendly cartoon faces for you. You only type the names.

Is this the same as the commercial Guess Who game?

No. It is an original template with its own characters. It uses the same simple idea of asking yes or no questions.

Can I use it in a classroom?

Yes. Teachers can use student names or historical figures, and the question cards include a classroom theme.
